## Details

### atas
- Details: ATAS (Any Time, Any Space) is a world-state-centric framework for agentic video generation, introduced in an arXiv preprint (2610.20571) by Wang et al. Instead of generating video frame-by-frame from a prompt, it maintains an explicit world state that the agent updates as it acts, letting it reason about any time and any space in the scene. The project page publishes video results, comparisons against other frameworks, and quantitative tables, plus BibTeX for citation. Useful for researchers and builders working on agentic or controllable video generation.

### harness
- Details: Harness² is Google Research's open-source framework for recursive agent harnessing — optimizing the system around a model (system prompt, guardrails, memory, tool guidance, subagents, skills, scripts, plugins) rather than the weights. Each step runs a contrastive Propose–Probe–Compose cycle: propose harness edits, probe their effects on the same task, and compose a refined harness from behavioral contrasts, without ground-truth feedback; a second recursion level accumulates evidence-supported lessons into a persistent improvement harness for future tasks. The repo reports gains of up to 11.5 points on WorkBuddy-Bench and 11.8 percentage points on LAB's partial pass rate for GLM-5 and Gemini 3.5 Flash (repo-reported, unverified). It supports parallel and sequential recursion with OpenCode and Codex executors across JobBench, LAB, and WorkBuddy-Bench, is Apache-2.0 licensed, and includes a no-credentials local demo.

### your-agent-harness-should-repair-itself
- Details: In "Your Agent Harness Should Repair Itself", Akshay Pachaar argues that current agent observability stops at "what happened" traces, leaving diagnosis, fixing, and prevention to developers on "developer time". The piece presents CometML's Opik as a self-correcting four-layer loop: tracing to capture failures, Ollie (a built-in coding agent) to diagnose them, plain-English test suites to lock expected behavior, and an agent sandbox to verify fixes — going from a bad trace to an approved fix, a rerun, and a locked regression test automatically. All outbound links in the article point to the open-source comet-ml/opik GitHub repo, which includes a self-host command.

### engy
- Details: Engy is an inference provider positioning itself as "the energy grid for an AI-native world", serving frontier open-weight models — DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, GLM 5.3/5.2, Kimi K3, Qwen3.x, MiniMax-H3 — through an OpenAI-compatible API. Its differentiators are cryptographic proof of correct inference, zero data retention, and per-token prices it claims undercut official APIs by up to 90% on a 3:1 input/output blend (vendor-claimed, unverified). Worth a look for cheap high-context inference (up to 1M tokens on several models), but treat the pricing and "verified inference" claims as marketing until independently tested.
