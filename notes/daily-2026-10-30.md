## Week: 22–29 Oct 2026

### 1. Ovis
- URL: https://github.com/devswha/ovis
- Posted by: cata, 29 Oct
- Category: ai-tool, github
- Brief: Ovis is a free, open-source browser and phone client for herdr (formerly the herdr web UI) that turns the coding agents on a user's computer into chats answerable with a click or tap, with a live terminal, workspaces across machines over SSH, and push alerts when an agent asks something or finishes. It covers Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode and more, runs on the user's own hardware with no account, and pairs the phone over Tailscale, a VPN or an SSH tunnel.
- Warning: anonymous usage counts are on by default (disableable in Settings or with OVIS_TELEMETRY=0); the phone can only reach a computer that is switched on.

### 2. What Effort Level Should I Choose?
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34491
- Posted by: asx, 29 Oct (via Zain Hasan's tweet; link in the author's reply below the post)
- Category: article
- Brief: This paper asks whether the reasoning-effort setting on today's models is worth what it costs, measuring the counterfactual effect of effort levels on proprietary and open-weight models across four benchmarks, single-call and long-horizon agentic tasks. It finds higher effort frequently fails tasks lower effort solved and costs up to 42x more on tasks every level can solve, and that starting low and escalating only after a verified failure matches or beats fixed top effort at lower cost; the trajectories behind the results are published alongside it (see Reasoning-Effort Trajectories).
- Warning: the $25,000 spend figure is the author's own claim in the tweet; the findings are the paper's measurements on its benchmarks, not a guarantee for any particular workload.

### 3. Reasoning-Effort Trajectories
- URL: https://huggingface.co/datasets/KaiserWhoLearns/ReasiningEffortTrajec
- Posted by: asx, 29 Oct (via Zain Hasan's tweet; link in the author's reply below the post)
- Category: web-app
- Brief: The trajectories behind the reasoning-effort paper: one run per model, benchmark and effort combination across eight models and four benchmarks (AIME 2026, GPQA Diamond, Terminal-Bench 2.1 and SWE-bench Pro), with per-attempt samples, resolution outcomes and trajectory files. It lets readers replay exactly what each effort level did — and what it cost — rather than trusting the paper's aggregates, and is the data behind "What Effort Level Should I Choose?".
- Warning: the release is licensed by subset because the source benchmarks carry different licences — check the subset licence before reuse. The dataset slug is spelled "ReasiningEffortTrajec" on Hugging Face, as published.
